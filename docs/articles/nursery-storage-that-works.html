Nursery Storage Ideas: How to Choose and Organise a Kids' Wardrobe

Baby clothes are outgrown fast and the stuff keeps piling up. A good storage setup can save you precious minutes every morning.

Why a kids' wardrobe?

A light plastic or cube wardrobe is popular because it is cheaper than wooden furniture, easy to assemble, easy to move and has open shelves so you can see everything at a glance. Parents use it for clothes, diapers, blankets and toys all in one place.

What to look for

  • Stability. Cube wardrobes can wobble. Check the weight limit and look at the build and reviews.
  • Safety. Anchor tall furniture to the wall if you can. Toddlers love to climb, and tip-overs are a real risk.
  • Smell. New plastic can have a strong smell. Air it out for a day or two before using.
  • Edges. Rounded corners and no sharp or loose parts.
  • Size. Measure the spot first, and make sure doors or flaps open without blocking the room.

How to organise it

  1. Heavy things low, light things high. Put blankets and heavy items at the bottom.
  2. Sort by size. Keep one shelf for the next size up so you can swap as baby grows.
  3. Everyday basics at eye level: onesies, nappies, wipes, cream.
  4. Outgrown clothes out. Bag them as soon as they get too small, then donate or pass on.
  5. Label the shelves so anyone in the family can find things at 3 am.

Small habits that help

  • Keep a "night kit" ready: diapers, wipes, a spare outfit, a muslin cloth.
  • Wash new clothes before baby wears them.
  • Do a quick sort every month, because babies change sizes faster than you expect.
