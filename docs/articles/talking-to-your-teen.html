Talking to your teen: how to start conversations that don't end in a fight

Teenage mein bachcha apni duniya banata hai, aur parents ko lagta hai ki wo door ho gaya hai. Sach ye hai ki teens ko aapki zaroorat pehle se zyada hoti hai, bas style alag chahiye.

Listen first

Most teens shut down at the first lecture. Try letting them finish speaking before you respond. Nod, ask "Phir kya hua?" and hold back advice unless they ask for it.

Pick the right moment

Big talks work better side by side than face to face: in the car, on a walk, while cooking or at a late-night snack. Less eye contact feels safer for many teens.

Ask open questions

Instead of "Exam kaisa gaya?", try "Aaj ka din sabse accha kya tha?" or "Kisi cheez ne tumhe pareshan kiya?" Open questions get more than one-word answers.

Respect their privacy and their rules

Teens are building independence. Agree on a few clear, fair rules together (curfew, phone use, study time) instead of setting every rule yourself. They keep what they helped build.

Stay calm about mistakes

If they tell you something hard, how you react decides whether they tell you the next thing. Thank them for being honest first, then talk about the problem.

Notice the warning signs

Changes that last for weeks, such as withdrawing from friends, sleeping too much or too little, big drops in grades, talk of feeling hopeless, or any mention of self-harm, need professional help. Talk to your doctor or a school or mental health counsellor soon. If your teen may be in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a local helpline right away.

Small things that build trust

  • Eat at least one meal together most days.
  • Say "I love you" and "I'm proud of you" out loud, even if they roll their eyes.
  • Apologise when you get it wrong. It teaches them to do the same.

General parenting information, not professional advice.
