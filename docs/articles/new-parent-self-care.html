New Parent Self-Care: Sleep, Support and When to Ask for Help

Share:WhatsAppCopy link

New parents look after the baby first and forget about themselves. But an exhausted parent cannot give their best, so taking care of yourself is not selfish.

Sleep in pieces

Unbroken sleep is rare in the first months. What helps: - Sleep when baby sleeps, even for 20 minutes, at least once a day. - Share the night. One partner does the first half, the other the second, so each gets a longer stretch. - Say yes when family offers to hold the baby while you nap.

Eat and drink

Keep water, fruit, nuts and easy snacks near your feeding spot. Skipping meals makes tiredness and mood worse. If you are breastfeeding, you need extra fluids and food.

Ask for and accept help

Make a short list for visitors: bring food, fold laundry, wash vessels, hold the baby while I shower. Most people want to help and just need to be told how.

Talk to your partner

Say what you need clearly, instead of expecting them to guess. Divide tasks by time (night shift, morning shift), not by "who is more tired".

Baby blues vs something more

Mood swings, tearfulness and anxiety in the first two weeks after birth are common and are often called baby blues. If sadness, anxiety, anger or feeling disconnected from your baby lasts longer than two weeks, gets worse, or you have thoughts of harming yourself or the baby, this may be postpartum depression or anxiety. It is common and treatable. Please speak to your doctor or midwife as soon as you can. Dads and partners can be affected too. If anyone is in immediate danger, call emergency services or a local helpline.

Little breaks that count

A 10-minute walk, a hot shower, a call with a friend, a favourite song. Small resets add up.
