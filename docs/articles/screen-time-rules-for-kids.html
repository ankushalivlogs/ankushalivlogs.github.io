Screen Time for Kids: Simple Family Rules That Work

Share:WhatsAppCopy link

Phones, tablets and TV: today's children grow up around screens. The goal is not to remove them completely, but to give them a healthy place in the day.

What guidelines suggest

Paediatric groups such as the AAP and WHO broadly suggest: - Under 18 months: avoid screens other than video calls with family. - Ages 2 to 5: about an hour a day at most, of good quality content, ideally watched together. - Ages 6 and up: set consistent limits so screens do not crowd out sleep, play, homework, meals and family time.

Rules that are easy to follow

  1. No screens at meals. Dinner is family talk time.
  2. No screens an hour before bed. Screens late in the evening can push bedtime later and hurt sleep.
  3. Screen-free zones, like bedrooms and the dining table.
  4. Pick the content together. Slow, age-suitable shows and educational apps beat endless autoplay videos.
  5. Use a timer so the end of screen time is the timer's job, not a fight with you.

Be the example

Kids copy what they see. If we scroll through meals, the rule will not stick. Keep your own phone away during family time.

Swap, don't just stop

Taking a screen away leaves a gap. Have easy alternatives ready: books, drawing, building blocks, outdoor games, helping in the kitchen, a quick dance party.

Not all screen time is equal

Video-calling grandparents, creating something, or learning a skill is very different from passive scrolling. Look at what they do on the screen, not just the minutes.

Signs to pay attention to

Big tantrums when screens are taken away, trouble sleeping, or losing interest in play and friends are signs to cut back and talk to your paediatrician or school counsellor.
