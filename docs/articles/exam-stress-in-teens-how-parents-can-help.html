Exam Stress in Teens: How Parents Can Help

Share:WhatsAppCopy link

When board exams come close, the mood at home gets heavy. How parents behave can raise or lower a teen's stress.

Signs of exam stress

  • Trouble sleeping or sleeping too much
  • Headaches, stomach aches or frequent illness
  • Irritability, crying or withdrawing
  • Losing appetite or eating too much
  • Saying "I'm going to fail" again and again

How to help

  1. Listen first. Ask "How are you feeling about it?" and let them talk without a lecture.
  2. Watch your expectations. Comparing with cousins or toppers adds fear. Praise effort, not only marks.
  3. Help with a plan. Break the syllabus into small daily goals and include breaks.
  4. Protect sleep. Teens need around 8 to 10 hours. All-night studying usually hurts memory and mood.
  5. Feed the brain. Regular meals, water, fruit and nuts. Go easy on caffeine and junk.
  6. Move a little. A walk or a game of badminton resets the mind.
  7. Keep the home calm. Lower the noise, keep routines, and avoid fights before the exam.

Things to say

"I believe in you." "Whatever the result, we are with you." These sentences lift more pressure than any tuition.

When to get help

If your teen has constant sadness, panic attacks, stops eating or sleeping, loses interest in everything, or says anything about self-harm, talk to your doctor or a school or mental health counsellor soon. If your teen may be in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a local helpline right away.

General parenting information, not professional advice.
