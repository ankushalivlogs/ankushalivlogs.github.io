# Baby Vaccination Schedule: Why It Matters and How to Keep Track
Category: Babies
Description: Why on-time baby vaccines matter, how to keep a vaccination card, what mild side effects are normal, and when to call the doctor.

Vaccines protect babies from serious illnesses. Remembering the schedule is a big job for parents, so these tips will help.

## Why vaccines matter
Vaccines train a baby's body to fight infections such as polio, measles, tuberculosis, hepatitis B, diphtheria, whooping cough and tetanus. Following the schedule gives protection when babies are most at risk.

## Follow your doctor's schedule
In India, doctors follow the national immunisation schedule and the guidance of the Indian Academy of Pediatrics. Your paediatrician will tell you which vaccines are due at which age, and which optional vaccines are worth considering.

## How to keep track
- Keep the **vaccination card** safe, and take it to every visit.
- Take a clear photo of every page as a backup.
- Set phone reminders for the next due date.
- Ask the clinic to note the next date on the card before you leave.

## On the day
- Feed baby before the visit and bring a comfort item.
- Wear clothes that are easy to open at the leg or arm.
- Stay for about 15 to 30 minutes after, in case of a rare reaction.

## Common, mild side effects
A little fussiness, a sore or red spot at the injection site, and a low fever for a day or two are common. Hold, feed and comfort baby. Ask the doctor about fever medicine and dose before giving any.

## Call the doctor if
Baby has a high fever, will not stop crying, is very drowsy or floppy, has a seizure, or has trouble breathing. Get emergency help for severe reactions.

## Missed a dose?
Don't panic. Call the clinic and ask how to catch up, since most schedules can be resumed.

*General information, not medical advice. Please follow your doctor's schedule.*
