# Starting solids: how to think about baby cereal
Products: cerelac

Chhe mahine ka hote hi sab log pooch ne lagte hain, "ab kya khilaana shuru karein?" Baby cereal like Cerelac is a popular first step because it is quick, smooth and fortified. But it is one option among several, and it helps to know how to use it well.

## When to start
Indian and global guidelines (WHO, IAP) generally advise exclusive breastfeeding or formula for the first 6 months, then adding complementary foods while milk feeds continue. Look for signs of readiness: baby sits with support, has good head control, shows interest in your food and no longer pushes food out with the tongue. Check timing with your paediatrician, especially for premature babies.

## Why parents like cereal
- **Smooth texture** that is easy for a first spoon.
- **Added iron and other nutrients**, useful because a baby's iron stores start dropping around 6 months.
- **Convenience** for travel, nights out or busy days.

## How to introduce it
1. Start with 1 to 2 spoons, thin and runny, mixed with breast milk, formula or boiled and cooled water as the pack says.
2. Offer one new food at a time and wait about 3 days before the next, so you can spot any reaction.
3. Increase thickness and quantity slowly as baby gets used to it.
4. Always feed with baby sitting upright and never leave them alone with food.

## What to watch out for
- **Read the label.** Different varieties have different ingredients, and some have added sugar. Choose what suits your baby's age and your doctor's advice.
- **No honey before 12 months**, and no added salt or sugar in home-cooked food for babies.
- **Do not replace milk** with cereal in the early weeks of solids. Milk is still the main food.
- **Allergy signs:** rash, swelling, vomiting or breathing trouble need urgent medical help.

## Mix it up
Cereal should not be the only thing for long. Slowly add home-cooked foods like dal water, mashed banana, moong dal khichdi, rice and vegetable purees. Varied tastes and textures help baby accept more foods later.

*General information from a parent, not medical advice. Your paediatrician knows your baby best.*
