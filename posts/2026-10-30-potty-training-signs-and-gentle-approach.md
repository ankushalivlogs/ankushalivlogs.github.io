# Potty Training: Signs of Readiness and a Gentle Approach
Category: Toddlers
Description: How to know your toddler is ready for potty training, plus a step-by-step gentle approach that avoids pressure and power struggles.
Products: mamy, diaper, pants

Potty training ka koi fixed din nahi hota. Bachche ki taiyari dekhna zyada zaroori hai, kyunki zabardasti ulta nuksaan karti hai.

## Signs your child is ready
Most children show readiness between 18 and 36 months. Look for:
- Staying dry for 2 hours or more
- Telling you they need to go, or showing signs like squirming or hiding
- Following simple instructions
- Walking to the potty and pulling pants up and down
- Showing interest in the toilet or in wearing underwear

## Getting started
1. **Pick a calm time.** Avoid starting during a move, a new baby or a big change.
2. **Introduce the potty** as a fun object first. Let them sit on it with clothes on.
3. **Set a routine.** Sit them on the potty after waking, after meals and before bath.
4. **Praise effort,** not just results. Stickers and cheers work well.
5. **Use easy clothes.** Pull-up pants and loose bottoms help.

## Accidents will happen
Stay calm, clean up and say "Koi baat nahi, next time potty mein." Punishing or shaming causes stress and delays training.

## Nights
Night dryness often comes months or even years after daytime training. Many children still need a diaper at night, and that is normal.

## Handy things
A small potty or toilet seat reducer, a stool for the toilet, easy-to-wash underwear, and spare clothes in your bag.

## When to talk to the doctor
If your child seems constipated, has pain when passing stool, or is still not ready by about age 4, check with your paediatrician.
