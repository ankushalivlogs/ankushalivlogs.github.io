# Baby Walkers vs Ride-On Toys: Are They Safe? What Parents Should Know
Description: Why paediatric experts warn against wheeled baby walkers, safer alternatives, and how to use ride-on toys safely.
Category: Toddlers
Products: walker, ride-on

A baby walker sounds like a shortcut to early walking, but the reality is quite different. That is why this article matters.

## The problem with wheeled walkers
Paediatric bodies, including the American Academy of Pediatrics, have raised strong concerns about wheeled baby walkers. Babies in walkers can move fast and reach things they could not reach before, which leads to falls down stairs, burns, poisoning and tip-overs. Walkers also do not help babies walk sooner, and some studies suggest they can delay it.

So if you are thinking about a walker, please talk to your paediatrician first.

## Safer alternatives
- **Floor time** on a play mat: the best way to build leg, back and core strength.
- **Stationary activity centres** that spin and bounce but do not move around the house.
- **Push toys** (a sturdy cart or walker-style toy) once baby can pull up and stand, supervised and away from stairs.
- **Holding baby's hands** and letting them cruise along furniture.

## Ride-on toy cars
A ride-on car is different from a walker. It is meant for toddlers who can already sit and walk steadily, usually around 12 months or older. Always follow the age and weight limits on the pack.

- Use only on flat, open floors, away from stairs, pools, balconies and roads.
- Stay close and supervise.
- Pick a low seat, wide wheels and a back support so it does not tip.
- Check there are no sharp edges or small loose parts.

In some of our video descriptions, an item may be labelled "baby walker" even though the product page shows a ride-on or activity toy. Please always read the product description, the age range and the safety notes on the store page before you buy.
